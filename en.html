01 — A COOL PAST

From mountain snow
to the scoop we love

The frozen treat we love has a story stretching back thousands of years, long before today's creamy scoops.

2000 BC

Ancient China

Stories tell of ice or snow served with milk, honey, and fruit in royal courts.

400 BC

Persian Faloodeh

Thin noodles, rose water, and ice formed an early ancestor of chilled desserts.

1533

A frozen treat travels to Europe

Chilled desserts became fashionable in European courts through travel and culinary exchange.

1843

The hand-cranked freezer

Nancy Johnson patented a hand-cranked ice cream freezer, making churning more practical.

1900s

Cold storage for everyone

Modern refrigeration helped ice cream reach homes and shops around the world.

Some early ice cream stories have competing versions, especially the Marco Polo legend.

02 — KNOW YOUR SCOOP

Ice cream cousins
what sets them apart?

They may look alike, but ingredients and texture give each style its own character.

American Ice Cream

Made with more cream and milk fat, and churned with more air for a fluffy, rich texture.

Creamy • airy

Italian Gelato

Milk-forward and churned with less air, so it feels dense and lets its flavors shine.

Dense • vivid

Sorbet & Sherbet

Sorbet contains no dairy; sherbet adds a little milk or cream to its fruity base.

Fruity • refreshing
Soft Serve & Scoop

Soft serve is churned and served right away; scoop ice cream is hardened for shaping.

Soft • scoopable

05 — COOL FACTS

Little facts
to chill with

🧠

Why does brain freeze happen?

A sudden chill on the roof of your mouth can stimulate nerves and cause a brief headache known as brain freeze.

How to ease it

Pause, press your tongue against the roof of your mouth, or sip room-temperature water.

📅

National Ice Cream Day

In the United States, National Ice Cream Day is celebrated on the third Sunday in July.

Ice Cream Month

President Ronald Reagan proclaimed July 1984 National Ice Cream Month.
